# Sixth Circuit Immigration Precedent — Quick Reference

## Purpose
Key Sixth Circuit immigration decisions organized by topic. This is a starting point — always search Midpage for the most current authority. The Sixth Circuit's immigration docket is active and precedent evolves.

**IMPORTANT:** This reference was compiled as a starting framework. Every case listed here MUST be verified through Midpage's citator before citing. Cases may have been distinguished, overruled, or superseded since this reference was written.

---

## Standard of Review

The Sixth Circuit reviews the BIA's factual findings under the **substantial evidence** standard. The court will reverse only if "any reasonable adjudicator would be compelled to conclude to the contrary." INA § 242(b)(4)(B).

Legal questions (including interpretation of the INA and regulations) are reviewed **de novo**, but the court generally accords *Chevron* deference to the BIA's reasonable interpretations of ambiguous statutory provisions.

**Practice note:** This is a high bar for petitioners. Frame petitions for review around legal error rather than factual disagreement where possible.

## Asylum / Withholding / CAT — Sixth Circuit

### Credibility
- The Sixth Circuit applies the REAL ID Act credibility standards. INA § 208(b)(1)(B)(iii).
- An adverse credibility finding must be supported by specific, cogent reasons that bear a legitimate nexus to the finding.
- Minor inconsistencies or omissions that do not go to the heart of the claim may not support an adverse credibility determination.

### Particular Social Group
- The Sixth Circuit applies the *M-E-V-G-* framework: immutability, particularity, social distinction.
- The court has been receptive to family-based PSG claims, especially post-*L-E-A- III*.
- **Research note:** Always search Midpage for the most recent Sixth Circuit PSG decisions — the court has addressed PSG formulations in numerous unpublished decisions that reveal patterns.

### Nexus
- The protected ground must be "at least one central reason" for the persecution. INA § 208(b)(1)(B)(i).
- The Sixth Circuit examines nexus carefully in mixed-motive cases (where both personal and protected-ground motivations exist).

### Country Conditions
- The Sixth Circuit expects specific, current country conditions evidence tied to the applicant's particular claim.
- Generic reports of instability or violence are insufficient without a connection to the applicant's protected ground.

### Government Unable or Unwilling
- For claims involving non-government persecutors, the applicant must show the government is unable or unwilling to control the persecutor.
- Evidence of reporting to authorities and their failure to act is strong evidence of government unwillingness.
- Pattern and practice of government inaction toward similarly situated individuals can also suffice.

## Cancellation of Removal — Sixth Circuit

### Stop-Time Rule
- *Niz-Chavez v. Garland*, 141 S. Ct. 1474 (2021) — An NTA must be a single document containing all required information (including time and place of hearing) to trigger the stop-time rule. This is binding Supreme Court precedent applied in the Sixth Circuit.
- **Practice note:** In the Sixth Circuit, always examine the NTA for completeness. If the NTA was served without a hearing date and a subsequent notice provided the date, the stop-time rule may not have been triggered, potentially making a client eligible for cancellation.

### Hardship
- The Sixth Circuit reviews the BIA's hardship determination under the substantial evidence standard and has generally been deferential.

## Petitions for Review — Procedure

### Filing Deadline
- Petition for review must be filed within **30 days** of the final order of removal. INA § 242(b)(1). This is jurisdictional and cannot be extended.

### Exhaustion
- Administrative remedies must be exhausted before seeking judicial review. INA § 242(d)(1). Issues not raised before the BIA are generally waived.

### Stay of Removal
- Filing a petition for review does NOT automatically stay removal. A separate motion for stay must be filed. INA § 242(b)(3)(B).
- The Sixth Circuit evaluates stays under the traditional four-factor test: likelihood of success on the merits, irreparable harm, balance of equities, and public interest.

### Scope of Review
- The Sixth Circuit can review final orders of removal, including constitutional claims and questions of law. INA § 242(a)(2)(D).
- The court generally cannot review discretionary decisions (e.g., the exercise of discretion in asylum cases), but can review whether the BIA applied the correct legal standard.

## Criminal Immigration Issues — Sixth Circuit

### Categorical Approach
- The Sixth Circuit applies the categorical and modified categorical approaches to determine whether a state conviction qualifies as an aggravated felony, CIMT, or other removable offense.
- *Descamps v. United States*, 570 U.S. 254 (2013) and *Mathis v. United States*, 579 U.S. 500 (2016) — Framework cases.

### Particularly Serious Crime
- The BIA has discretion to determine whether a crime is "particularly serious" for purposes of the withholding bar. The Sixth Circuit reviews this determination for abuse of discretion.

## Due Process — Sixth Circuit

### Ineffective Assistance of Counsel
- The Sixth Circuit applies the *Matter of Lozada*, 19 I&N Dec. 637 (BIA 1988), requirements for ineffective assistance claims:
  1. Affidavit detailing the agreement with counsel and how counsel failed
  2. Counsel must be informed of the allegations and given opportunity to respond
  3. Complaint filed with disciplinary authorities (or explanation of why not)

### Notice Issues
- Defective NTA issues (missing time/place of hearing) — see *Pereira* and *Niz-Chavez* above.
- In absentia orders may be challenged if notice was defective.

## Research Protocol for Sixth Circuit Cases

When researching Sixth Circuit immigration precedent:

1. **Search Midpage first** with jurisdiction filter set to Sixth Circuit
2. **Check published vs. unpublished**: Published opinions are binding precedent. Unpublished opinions are not binding but can be cited as persuasive authority under Sixth Circuit Rule 32.1.
3. **Check for en banc decisions** that may have modified or overruled panel decisions
4. **Check Supreme Court grants of certiorari** — a case pending cert review may be unreliable authority
5. **Note the panel composition** — while not formally relevant, understanding which judges authored key decisions can inform strategic assessment
6. **Always cross-reference with BIA precedent** — the BIA's position on an issue and the Sixth Circuit's position may differ, which matters depending on the procedural posture
