## 1. Asylum — INA § 208, 8 U.S.C. § 1158

### Statutory Framework
A refugee is a person who is unable or unwilling to return to their country of nationality because of persecution or a well-founded fear of persecution on account of race, religion, nationality, membership in a particular social group, or political opinion. INA § 101(a)(42)(A), 8 U.S.C. § 1101(a)(42)(A).

### Standard
- **Well-founded fear** = reasonable possibility of persecution. *INS v. Cardoza-Fonseca*, 480 U.S. 421, 440 (1987) (even a 10% chance can suffice).
- **Past persecution** creates a rebuttable presumption of well-founded fear. 8 C.F.R. § 1208.13(b)(1).
- **Nexus**: Persecution must be "on account of" a protected ground. The protected ground need not be the sole or central reason, but must be "at least one central reason." INA § 208(b)(1)(B)(i); *Matter of J-B-N- & S-M-*, 24 I&N Dec. 208 (BIA 2007).

### Elements (applicant must establish all):
1. Suffered past persecution OR has a well-founded fear of future persecution
2. Persecution is on account of a protected ground (race, religion, nationality, PSG, political opinion)
3. Nexus between the harm and the protected ground
4. Government is the persecutor OR the government is unable or unwilling to control the persecutor
5. No firm resettlement in a third country
6. Filed within one year of arrival or qualifies for an exception
7. Merits a favorable exercise of discretion

## 2. Withholding of Removal — INA § 241(b)(3), 8 U.S.C. § 1231(b)(3)

### Standard
- **Clear probability** = more likely than not that the applicant's life or freedom would be threatened on account of a protected ground. *INS v. Stevic*, 467 U.S. 407 (1984).
- Higher standard than asylum.
- Mandatory relief — no discretionary component (if elements are met, relief must be granted).
- No one-year filing deadline.
- Cannot be granted if the applicant has been convicted of a "particularly serious crime." INA § 241(b)(3)(B).

## 3. Convention Against Torture (CAT) — 8 C.F.R. § 1208.16(c), § 1208.17, § 1208.18

### Standard
- **More likely than not** that the applicant would be tortured if removed to the proposed country of removal.
- No nexus to a protected ground required.
- "Torture" = intentional infliction of severe pain or suffering, by or at the instigation of or with the consent or acquiescence of a public official. 8 C.F.R. § 1208.18(a)(1).
- "Acquiescence" = willful blindness. The public official need not have actual knowledge but must have been aware of a substantial likelihood of torture occurring. *Matter of W-G-R-*, 26 I&N Dec. 208 (BIA 2014), vacated and remanded in part on other grounds.

### Two forms:
- **CAT withholding** (non-refoulement): Cannot be removed to the country where torture would occur. Can be terminated if conditions change.
- **CAT deferral**: For those subject to mandatory bars. More limited protection; can be terminated more easily.

## 4. Key BIA Precedent Decisions

**Always verify citator status before citing these. BIA precedent evolves rapidly, especially after AG certification.**

### Particular Social Group
- *Matter of Acosta*, 19 I&N Dec. 211 (BIA 1985) — Foundational PSG case: immutable characteristic test
- *Matter of M-E-V-G-*, 26 I&N Dec. 227 (BIA 2014) — PSG must be (1) immutable, (2) particular, (3) socially distinct
- *Matter of W-G-R-*, 26 I&N Dec. 208 (BIA 2014) — Companion to M-E-V-G-; social distinction analyzed
- *Matter of L-E-A-*, 27 I&N Dec. 581 (A.G. 2019) — Family-based PSG narrowed by AG; *check subsequent history carefully*
- *Matter of L-E-A- III*, 28 I&N Dec. 304 (A.G. 2021) — Vacated prior AG decision; restored BIA framework for family-based PSG
- *Matter of A-B-*, 27 I&N Dec. 316 (A.G. 2018) — Narrowed domestic violence and gang-based PSG claims
- *Matter of A-B- II*, 28 I&N Dec. 199 (A.G. 2021) — Further restricted PSG claims
- *Matter of A-B- III*, 28 I&N Dec. 307 (A.G. 2021) — Garland AG vacated *A-B- I* and *A-B- II*; restored pre-*A-B-* framework

### Nexus
- *Matter of J-B-N- & S-M-*, 24 I&N Dec. 208 (BIA 2007) — "At least one central reason" standard for nexus

### Past Persecution
- *Matter of L-S-*, 25 I&N Dec. 705 (BIA 2012) — Standard for establishing past persecution; severity threshold

### Internal Relocation
- *Matter of M-Z-M-R-*, 26 I&N Dec. 28 (BIA 2012) — Internal relocation analysis

### Credibility
- *Matter of J-Y-C-*, 24 I&N Dec. 260 (BIA 2007) — Credibility standards under REAL ID Act

## 5. Sixth Circuit Key Precedent

**Immigration cases in the Sixth Circuit — always cite these when applicable:**

### Standard of Review
- The Sixth Circuit reviews factual findings (including credibility) under the substantial evidence standard: the BIA's findings are "conclusive unless any reasonable adjudicator would be compelled to conclude to the contrary." INA § 242(b)(4)(B); *Zhao v. Holder*, 569 F.3d 238, 247 (6th Cir. 2009).

### PSG in the Sixth Circuit
- The Sixth Circuit applies the *M-E-V-G-* framework (immutability, particularity, social distinction) but has been receptive to a range of PSG formulations.
- **Research note:** Always search Midpage for the most recent Sixth Circuit PSG cases — this area evolves rapidly.

### Nexus
- The Sixth Circuit requires the protected ground to be "at least one central reason" for the persecution, consistent with the REAL ID Act.

### Country Conditions
- The Sixth Circuit has emphasized that country conditions evidence must be specific to the applicant's circumstances, not just general reports of instability. Generic reports without connection to the applicant's specific claim are insufficient.

### Corroboration
- Under the REAL ID Act, the IJ may require corroboration of otherwise credible testimony if it is reasonable to expect it. *Gaye v. Lynch*, 788 F.3d 519 (6th Cir. 2015).

## 6. Particular Social Group Framework — Current State

The current framework requires three elements (per *M-E-V-G-* and *W-G-R-*):

1. **Immutability (or Fundamental):** Members share a characteristic that is either innate, beyond the power of the individual to change, or so fundamental to identity or conscience that it ought not be required to be changed. *Matter of Acosta*, 19 I&N Dec. at 233.

2. **Particularity:** The group must be defined with sufficient particularity to not be amorphous, overbroad, diffuse, or subjective. *Matter of M-E-V-G-*, 26 I&N Dec. at 239.

3. **Social Distinction:** The group must be perceived as a group by the society in question. This is a societal-perception test, not a visibility test. The group need not be "on sight" recognizable. *Matter of M-E-V-G-*, 26 I&N Dec. at 238.

**Post-*A-B- III* landscape:** The AG vacated *A-B- I* and *A-B- II*, restoring the pre-2018 framework. Domestic violence and gang-based PSG claims are evaluated under the standard *M-E-V-G-* framework without the additional restrictions imposed by *A-B- I/II*. However, these claims still must satisfy all three PSG elements.

**Practice note:** PSG must be defined independently of the persecution. A group defined by the harm itself (e.g., "people who are persecuted") is circular and will be rejected. *Matter of M-E-V-G-*, 26 I&N Dec. at 236 n.11.

## 7. One-Year Filing Deadline — INA § 208(a)(2)(B)

Asylum applications must be filed within one year of the applicant's arrival in the United States, unless the applicant demonstrates:

- **Changed circumstances** materially affecting eligibility (e.g., changed country conditions, change in personal circumstances), OR
- **Extraordinary circumstances** relating to the delay (e.g., serious illness, mental disability, ineffective assistance of prior counsel, maintained lawful status)

The application must be filed within a reasonable period given those circumstances. 8 C.F.R. § 1208.4(a)(4)-(5).

**Practice note:** The one-year bar does not apply to withholding of removal or CAT claims. If the one-year deadline is missed and no exception applies, shift analysis to withholding and CAT.

## 8. Discretionary Factors — Asylum Only

Asylum is discretionary even if all elements are met. Favorable factors include:

- Severity of past persecution
- Ties to the U.S. (family, employment, community)
- Evidence of good moral character
- Immigration history (lawful entry, no prior violations)

Adverse factors include:
- Criminal history (even if not a statutory bar)
- Immigration violations
- Failure to apply in a timely manner (even within the one-year deadline)
- Fraud or misrepresentation (if not rising to a statutory bar)

## 9. Bars to Asylum and Withholding

### Mandatory Bars to Asylum — INA § 208(b)(2)
- Persecution of others
- Conviction of a particularly serious crime (aggravated felony is per se particularly serious for asylum)
- Commission of a serious nonpolitical crime outside the U.S.
- Danger to the security of the U.S.
- Firm resettlement in a third country
- Terrorist activity / organization membership

### Bars to Withholding — INA § 241(b)(3)(B)
- Same as asylum bars, with a higher threshold for "particularly serious crime" (separate analysis required for withholding; an aggravated felony is not automatically a bar)

### CAT — No bars
CAT protection cannot be denied based on criminal history or any other bar. If the applicant establishes it is more likely than not they would be tortured, relief must be granted (though it may be granted as deferral rather than withholding for those subject to mandatory bars).

## 10. Burden of Proof and Standard of Review

| Forum | Burden | Standard of Review |
|-------|--------|-------------------|
| Immigration Judge | Applicant bears burden | De novo fact-finding |
| BIA | Applicant bears burden | Reviews IJ factual findings for clear error; legal questions de novo |
| Sixth Circuit | Petitioner bears burden | Substantial evidence (reversed only if "any reasonable adjudicator would be compelled to conclude to the contrary") |
| Supreme Court | Petitioner bears burden | Reviews circuit court legal conclusions de novo |

**Key practice point:** The substantial evidence standard at the circuit level is highly deferential. To prevail on a petition for review, the record must compel a different conclusion — not merely support one.
