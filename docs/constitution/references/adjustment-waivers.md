## 1. Adjustment of Status — INA § 245, 8 U.S.C. § 1255

### General Requirements:
1. Applicant is physically present in the U.S.
2. Applicant was "inspected and admitted" or "inspected and paroled" into the U.S. (with exceptions under INA § 245(i))
3. An immigrant visa is immediately available (priority date is current)
4. Applicant is admissible to the U.S. (or obtains a waiver of inadmissibility)
5. Applicant merits a favorable exercise of discretion

### INA § 245(i) Exception:
Allows adjustment for applicants who entered without inspection (EWI) IF they are the beneficiary of a petition or labor certification filed on or before April 30, 2001, and they were physically present in the U.S. on December 21, 2000. Requires payment of a $1,000 penalty. This is a critical exception for many long-residing immigrants.

### Immediate Relatives vs. Preference Categories:
- **Immediate relatives** (spouses, parents, and unmarried children under 21 of U.S. citizens): No visa number wait; visa always immediately available. INA § 201(b)(2)(A)(i).
- **Preference categories**: Subject to per-country limits and visa bulletin priority dates. Must wait for priority date to become current.

### Key Bars to Adjustment:
- Unauthorized employment (INA § 245(c)(2)) — waived for immediate relatives of U.S. citizens
- Failure to maintain lawful status (INA § 245(c)(2)) — waived for immediate relatives of U.S. citizens
- Entry without inspection (unless § 245(i) applies)
- Certain criminal grounds of inadmissibility (may require waiver)
- Prior removal order (may require I-212)

## 2. Key Grounds of Inadmissibility — INA § 212(a)

### Health-Related — § 212(a)(1)
- Communicable diseases, lack of required vaccinations, mental or physical disorder with harmful behavior
- **Waiver:** INA § 212(g) for certain health grounds

### Criminal — § 212(a)(2)
- Crime involving moral turpitude (CIMT) — § 212(a)(2)(A)(i)(I)
- Controlled substance violation — § 212(a)(2)(A)(i)(II)
- Multiple criminal convictions — § 212(a)(2)(B)
- Drug trafficking — § 212(a)(2)(C)
- **Petty offense exception:** One CIMT with max penalty ≤ 1 year AND actual sentence ≤ 6 months. § 212(a)(2)(A)(ii)(II).
- **Waiver:** INA § 212(h) for certain criminal grounds (requires qualifying relative for some categories)

### Immigration Violations — § 212(a)(6)
- Presence without admission or parole — § 212(a)(6)(A)
- Fraud or willful misrepresentation — § 212(a)(6)(C)(i)
- **Waiver for fraud/misrepresentation:** INA § 212(i) (I-601) — requires extreme hardship to qualifying U.S. citizen or LPR relative

### Unlawful Presence — § 212(a)(9)(B)
- **3-year bar:** 180 days to 1 year of unlawful presence, then departure → 3-year bar
- **10-year bar:** 1 year or more of unlawful presence, then departure → 10-year bar
- **Waiver:** INA § 212(a)(9)(B)(v) — requires extreme hardship to U.S. citizen or LPR spouse or parent (not children)
- **Provisional waiver (I-601A):** Available before departure for consular processing

### Prior Removal Orders — § 212(a)(9)(A)
- **5-year bar** after removal
- **10-year bar** after removal if aggravated felony or second removal
- **20-year bar** after second removal
- **Waiver:** INA § 212(a)(9)(A)(iii) — Permission to reapply (I-212)

## 3. I-601 Waiver — INA § 212(i)

### Purpose:
Waives inadmissibility based on fraud or willful misrepresentation under INA § 212(a)(6)(C)(i).

### Standard:
Applicant must demonstrate that refusal of admission would result in **extreme hardship** to a qualifying relative who is a U.S. citizen or LPR. The qualifying relative is limited to the applicant's spouse or parent (NOT children for § 212(i) purposes).

### Extreme Hardship Factors (*Matter of Cervantes-Gonzalez*, 22 I&N Dec. 560 (BIA 1999)):
- Health conditions of qualifying relative
- Financial impact on qualifying relative
- Education disruption
- Personal considerations (family ties, length of residence, community ties)
- Country conditions in the applicant's home country as they affect the qualifying relative
- Immigration consequences to the qualifying relative

**Practice note:** Extreme hardship must be assessed under two scenarios: (1) if the qualifying relative remains in the U.S. without the applicant, and (2) if the qualifying relative relocates abroad with the applicant. Hardship under at least one scenario must be established, but addressing both strengthens the case.

## 4. I-601A Provisional Unlawful Presence Waiver

### Purpose:
Allows applicants who are inadmissible only for unlawful presence (§ 212(a)(9)(B)) to obtain a provisional waiver BEFORE departing the U.S. for consular processing. This reduces the time spent abroad to attend the consular interview.

### Eligibility:
1. Physically present in the U.S.
2. 17 years of age or older
3. Inadmissible only under § 212(a)(9)(B)(i)(I) or (II)
4. Is the beneficiary of an approved immigrant visa petition (I-130)
5. Has a case pending with the Department of State for immigrant visa processing
6. Can demonstrate extreme hardship to a qualifying relative (U.S. citizen or LPR spouse or parent)

### Standard:
Same extreme hardship standard as the I-601 waiver — hardship to U.S. citizen or LPR spouse or parent.

### Key Practice Points:
- The I-601A is filed with USCIS while the applicant is still in the U.S.
- If approved, the applicant departs for their consular interview with the waiver already in hand
- If the applicant is found inadmissible on grounds other than unlawful presence at the consular interview, a separate waiver (I-601) may be needed
- The applicant must not have a final order of removal

## 5. I-212 Permission to Reapply — INA § 212(a)(9)(A)(iii)

### Purpose:
Allows an applicant who has been previously removed to apply for readmission before the statutory bar period has elapsed.

### Factors (*Matter of Tin*, 14 I&N Dec. 371 (Reg. Comm. 1973); *Matter of Lee*, 17 I&N Dec. 275 (Comm. 1978)):
- Basis for deportation/removal
- Recency of removal
- Length of residence in the U.S.
- Moral character
- Family ties in the U.S.
- Need for the applicant's services in the U.S.
- Respect for law and order
- Evidence of reformation and rehabilitation (if criminal grounds)
- Hardship to applicant and others
- Whether the applicant is admissible on all other grounds

## 6. Key BIA and Circuit Precedent

### Extreme Hardship
- *Matter of Cervantes-Gonzalez*, 22 I&N Dec. 560 (BIA 1999) — Foundational extreme hardship factors
- *Matter of Recinas*, 23 I&N Dec. 467 (BIA 2002) — Hardship analysis (note: this is the "exceptional and extremely unusual" standard for cancellation, which is higher than "extreme hardship" for waivers)

### Fraud/Misrepresentation
- *Matter of D-R-*, 25 I&N Dec. 445 (BIA 2011) — Elements of fraud and willful misrepresentation; materiality standard
- *Kungys v. United States*, 485 U.S. 759 (1988) — Materiality test for misrepresentation

### INA § 245(i)
- *Matter of Akrap*, 24 I&N Dec. 912 (BIA 2009) — Grandfathering under § 245(i)

## 7. Common Issues and Pitfalls

- **Confusing "extreme hardship" with "exceptional and extremely unusual hardship."** Extreme hardship (waiver standard) is LOWER than exceptional and extremely unusual hardship (non-LPR cancellation standard). Do not apply the wrong standard.
- **Qualifying relative errors.** Different waivers have different qualifying relatives. For the unlawful presence waiver (§ 212(a)(9)(B)(v)), children do NOT qualify — only U.S. citizen or LPR spouse or parent. For the fraud waiver (§ 212(i)), only U.S. citizen or LPR spouse or parent.
- **Failure to address both hardship scenarios.** USCIS expects analysis of hardship both if the qualifying relative stays in the U.S. and if they relocate. Address both.
- **Overlooking additional grounds of inadmissibility.** An applicant may have multiple grounds. A waiver of one ground does not cure others. Always do a complete inadmissibility analysis before filing.
- **INA § 245(i) grandfathering.** This is a narrow exception with strict date requirements. Verify the underlying petition filing date and the applicant's physical presence on December 21, 2000.
- **Prior removal orders.** A prior removal order creates a separate bar requiring an I-212 in addition to any other waiver. Don't forget to address it.
- **USCIS vs. consular processing.** The procedural path (adjustment vs. consular processing) affects which waivers are needed and when. An applicant who can adjust under § 245(i) may avoid triggering the unlawful presence bars entirely. Always analyze the procedural path first.
