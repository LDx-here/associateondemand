# Country Conditions Research — Source Guide

## Purpose
This reference guides the associate in locating, evaluating, and citing country conditions evidence for asylum, withholding, and CAT claims. Country conditions evidence is critical — courts have rejected claims with credible testimony where country conditions evidence was insufficient or outdated.

---

## Source Hierarchy (by evidentiary weight)

Courts generally accord the most weight to U.S. government sources, followed by international organizations, then NGOs, then academic and media sources. Within each tier, specificity to the applicant's claim matters more than the source's general prestige.

### Tier 1 — U.S. Government Sources
These carry the most weight because courts treat them as authoritative and relatively impartial.

**U.S. Department of State — Country Reports on Human Rights Practices**
- URL: https://www.state.gov/reports-bureau-of-democracy-human-rights-and-labor/country-reports-on-human-rights-practices/
- Published annually (usually March/April for the prior calendar year)
- Covers civil liberties, political freedoms, discrimination, rule of law, labor rights
- Most frequently cited source in asylum cases
- Always cite the most recent edition unless historical conditions are relevant

**U.S. Department of State — International Religious Freedom Reports**
- URL: https://www.state.gov/international-religious-freedom-reports/
- Published annually
- Essential for religion-based persecution claims

**U.S. Department of State — Trafficking in Persons Reports**
- URL: https://www.state.gov/trafficking-in-persons-report/
- Published annually
- Relevant for trafficking-related claims, CAT cases involving trafficking victims

**Congressional Research Service (CRS) Reports**
- URL: https://crsreports.congress.gov/
- Topical reports on specific countries and issues
- Well-regarded as nonpartisan analysis

**U.S. Commission on International Religious Freedom (USCIRF)**
- URL: https://www.uscirf.gov/
- Annual reports and country-specific recommendations
- Designates Countries of Particular Concern (CPC) and Special Watch List countries

**CIA World Factbook**
- URL: https://www.cia.gov/the-world-factbook/
- Background information on country demographics, government, economy
- Useful for context but rarely sufficient as primary country conditions evidence

### Tier 2 — International Organization Sources
Authoritative and generally well-received by courts.

**UNHCR (United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees)**
- URL: https://www.unhcr.org/
- Eligibility guidelines for specific countries (highly relevant)
- Country-specific guidance notes
- UNHCR position papers on particular groups
- Refworld database: https://www.refworld.org/ — aggregates country conditions sources

**UN Human Rights Council — Universal Periodic Review (UPR)**
- Country-specific human rights reviews
- Reports from UN Special Rapporteurs

**UN Office on Drugs and Crime (UNODC)**
- Relevant for claims involving criminal organizations, gangs, drug trafficking

**International Organization for Migration (IOM)**
- Country-specific migration and displacement data

### Tier 3 — NGO Sources
Well-known organizations whose reports are routinely cited in immigration proceedings.

**Amnesty International**
- URL: https://www.amnesty.org/
- Annual reports and country-specific urgent actions
- Detailed reports on specific human rights issues

**Human Rights Watch**
- URL: https://www.hrw.org/
- World Report (annual) and country-specific reports
- Often more detailed than State Department reports on specific issues

**Freedom House**
- URL: https://freedomhouse.org/
- Freedom in the World (annual country ratings)
- Freedom on the Net (internet freedom)
- Nations in Transit (post-communist countries)

**Reporters Without Borders (RSF)**
- URL: https://rsf.org/
- Press freedom index and country-specific reports
- Essential for political opinion claims involving journalists or media

**International Crisis Group**
- URL: https://www.crisisgroup.org/
- In-depth conflict analysis and country reports

**Transparency International**
- URL: https://www.transparency.org/
- Corruption Perceptions Index
- Relevant for claims involving government corruption and inability to protect

### Tier 4 — Regional and Country-Specific Sources
These provide granular detail that broader sources may lack.

**UK Home Office — Country Policy and Information Notes (CPINs)**
- URL: https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/country-policy-and-information-notes
- Detailed assessments for specific claim types in specific countries
- Highly useful for PSG-based claims (gender, LGBTQ, etc.)

**Canadian Immigration and Refugee Board — National Documentation Packages**
- URL: https://irb.gc.ca/en/country-information/Pages/index.aspx
- Comprehensive country conditions compilations
- Well-organized by topic

**Australian Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade — Country Information Reports**
- Detailed assessments used by Australian refugee tribunals

### Tier 5 — Academic and Media Sources
Supplementary. Should corroborate, not replace, the sources above.

**Academic sources:**
- Peer-reviewed journal articles
- University research center reports
- Expert declarations from country conditions experts

**Media sources:**
- Major international news organizations (Reuters, AP, BBC, etc.)
- Local media from the country in question (with translation if needed)
- **Caution:** News articles are corroborative evidence, not primary country conditions evidence. Courts have criticized reliance on news articles alone.

---

## Research Protocol

1. **Start with the most recent State Department Country Report.** This is your baseline. If it addresses the specific issue, it's your strongest source.

2. **Layer with UNHCR eligibility guidelines** if they exist for the country/claim type. These are often the most directly useful because they analyze claims by protected ground.

3. **Fill gaps with NGO reports.** If the State Department report doesn't address the specific persecution ground in enough detail, Human Rights Watch and Amnesty International often have more granular coverage.

4. **Use UK CPINs for claim-specific analysis.** These are particularly good for LGBTQ claims, domestic violence claims, and other PSG-based claims because they analyze specific claim types.

5. **Search for recent news** to ensure you're not missing a significant recent development (election, coup, policy change, new legislation).

6. **Check Refworld** (refworld.org) as a catch-all — it aggregates many of the above sources.

---

## Citation Format for Country Conditions Sources

Follow Bluebook 21st Edition for government reports and international organization documents:

**State Department Reports:**
U.S. Dep't of State, [Country] [Year] Human Rights Report [page or section] ([Year]).

**UNHCR:**
U.N. High Comm'r for Refugees, [Title] [page or paragraph] ([Date]).

**NGO Reports:**
[Organization], [Title] [page or section] ([Date]).

**News Articles:**
[Author], [Title], [Publication] ([Date]), [URL].

**Always include the publication date.** Courts discount stale evidence. A 2019 country report in a 2026 case will draw scrutiny — always explain why older sources remain relevant or supplement with current evidence.

---

## Red Flags — When Country Conditions Evidence is Weak

- **Only one source.** Courts expect multiple independent sources corroborating the same conditions.
- **All sources are more than 2 years old.** Supplement with recent evidence or explain why conditions haven't changed.
- **Generic reports without connection to the applicant's specific claim.** "Guatemala has high crime" doesn't support a PSG-based asylum claim. You need evidence specific to the persecution ground.
- **Reliance on news articles alone.** News articles should supplement, not replace, official reports.
- **Missing the most obvious source.** If there's a State Department Country Report for the country and year, and you didn't cite it, that's a gap the IJ or BIA will notice.
