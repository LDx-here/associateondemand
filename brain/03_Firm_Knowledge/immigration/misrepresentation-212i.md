# Misrepresentation & § 212(i) waiver

**Cite:** INA §§ 212(a)(6)(C)(i), 212(i); 8 USCIS-PM; 9 FAM 302.9
**Practice areas:** Waiver | Consular | AOS | Visa fraud

## What this element requires
- Willful misrepresentation of a **material** fact to procure a visa, admission, or other INA benefit
- Materiality: misrepresentation tends to shut off a line of inquiry relevant to eligibility
- Timely voluntary retraction may cure in some inspection contexts (narrow; fact-specific)
- **§ 212(i) waiver:** extreme hardship to USC/LPR **spouse or parent** + discretion

## Facts that prove it
- Statement / omission → visa DS-160 answers, border secondary, prior apps
- Benefit sought → tourist visa, EAD, AOS, entry
- Qualifying relative hardship → see `extreme-hardship-factors.md`
- Equities / remorse → corrected statements, consistent later filings

## Common pitfalls
- Confusing § 212(a)(6)(C)(i) misrepresentation with **false USC claim** (ii) — different waiver rules
- Assuming “honest mistake” always defeats willfulness without record support
- Filing AOS without addressing prior visa fraud disclosed on forms

## Sample language pointers
- Separate: (1) whether ground applies; (2) waiver eligibility; (3) hardship; (4) discretion
- Never coach clients to conceal prior misrepresentations
