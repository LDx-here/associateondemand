# Aggravated felony — overview

**Cite:** INA § 101(a)(43); §§ 237(a)(2)(A)(iii), 208(b)(2), 241(a)(5) consequences
**Practice areas:** Removal | Asylum bars | Cancellation | Naturalization

## What this element requires
- Offense must match a § 101(a)(43) category after categorical analysis
- Consequences often severe: deportability; asylum bar (AF = particularly serious for asylum); limited relief; permanent § 212(a)(9)(A) bar if removed as AF
- Sentence length sometimes statutory (e.g., theft/crime of violence with ≥1 year) — suspended sentence may still “count” depending on category
- Retroactivity and deportability-trigger dates can be circuit-sensitive — verify before advising

## Facts that prove it
- Exact statute + record of conviction
- Imposed sentence / confinement
- Date of conviction vs entry / adjustment / naturalization filing
- Post-conviction relief status (vacatur grounds matter)

## Common pitfalls
- Equating any felony with aggravated felony
- Missing state misdemeanors that still meet AF definitions via sentence
- Advising asylum/cancellation without AF screen

## Sample language pointers
- State hypothesized (a)(43) subsection and why elements match/fail
- Separate AF conclusion from CIMT / controlled-substance analysis
