# Extreme hardship (qualifying relative)

**Cite:** INA §§ 212(h), 212(i), 212(a)(9)(B)(v); 9 USCIS-PM B; Matter of Cervantes-Gonzalez
**Practice areas:** Waiver | AOS | Consular | Cancellation (related equities)

## What this element requires
- Qualifying relative: usually USC/LPR **spouse or parent** (check waiver statute — § 212(h) also allows child/son/daughter in some paths)
- Hardship if applicant is refused admission / removed — separation **or** relocation scenarios
- Cumulative weighing — one factor need not alone prove “extreme”
- Distinct from ordinary hardship of family separation; must be elevated

## Facts that prove it
- Medical → diagnoses, treatment plans, caregiver role, country-of-origin care gaps
- Financial → income share, debts, housing, inability to support two households
- Family/caregiving → minor children, elderly parents, special-needs dependents
- Country conditions → safety, employment, education, language barriers for QR
- Emotional/psychological → clinician letters; children’s school/IEP impacts
- Length of residence / community ties of QR in the U.S.

## Common pitfalls
- Arguing applicant’s own hardship as if it were QR hardship
- Listing ordinary inconvenience without elevation evidence
- Ignoring relocation vs separation theory consistency
- No corroboration (medical, school, financial docs)

## Sample language pointers
- Label each factor; end with cumulative totality sentence
- Address both separation and relocation if facts support either path
- Cross-walk exhibits: “See Ex. [letter/medical]”
