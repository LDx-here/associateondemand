# AOS statutory eligibility (§ 245)

**Cite:** INA §§ 245(a), 245(c), 245(i); 8 CFR § 245.1; USCIS-PM Vol. 7
**Practice areas:** AOS | Family | Employment | SIJS | VAWA

## What this element requires
- Immigrant visa immediately available (Visa Bulletin / USCIS chart guidance)
- Admissible under INA § 212(a), or eligible for and granted a waiver
- **§ 245(a):** inspected and admitted **or** paroled
- **§ 245(c) bars** (preference categories): unlawful status at filing; failure to maintain status; unauthorized employment; certain VWP/transit/crewman entries
- **Immediate relatives / VAWA / SIJS:** broad exemptions from most § 245(c) bars
- **§ 245(i):** grandfathering via approvable-when-filed petition/labor cert on or before **Apr 30, 2001** (+ physical presence Dec 21, 2000 if petition after Jan 14, 1998); I-485 Supp. A + penalty fee

## Facts that prove it
- Manner of entry → I-94, passport stamps, parole docs, credible EWI timeline
- Status history → visa, COS/EOS, overstay dates, work authorization
- Petition history → I-130/I-140/I-360/I-526 or labor cert filing date; approval/priority date
- Relationship → marriage/birth certificates; IR vs preference category
- § 245(i) presence → Dec 21, 2000 evidence (school, medical, tax, affidavits)

## Common pitfalls
- Assuming admission = inspected entry with false USC claim (not a lawful admission)
- Preference beneficiary barred by § 245(c) when IR conversion (e.g., LPR spouse naturalizes) would cure
- Triggering § 212(a)(9)(B) by consular departure without provisional waiver analysis
- Filing without visa availability / wrong Visa Bulletin chart

## Sample language pointers
- State pathway: “§ 245(a) as immediate relative” vs “§ 245(i) grandfathered”
- Separate eligibility, admissibility, and discretion into distinct sections
