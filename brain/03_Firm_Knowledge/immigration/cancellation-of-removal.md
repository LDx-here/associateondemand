# Cancellation of removal

**Cite:** INA §§ 240A(a) (LPR), 240A(b) (non-LPR); 8 CFR § 1240.20
**Practice areas:** Removal defense | Cancellation

## What this element requires
- **LPR cancellation (§ 240A(a)):** LPR ≥5 years; continuous residence ≥7 years after lawful admission; not convicted of aggravated felony; discretion
- **Non-LPR cancellation (§ 240A(b)):** continuous physical presence ≥10 years; GMC; not barred convictions; exceptional and extremely unusual hardship to USC/LPR spouse, parent, or child; discretion; annual caps
- Stop-time rules for certain offenses / service of NTA — verify current Supreme Court / BIA rules before advising
- Distinct from former § 212(c) (limited legacy cases only)

## Facts that prove it
- Admission / LPR card history; presence evidence (rent, school, medical, taxes)
- Qualifying relative hardship (non-LPR) — elevated standard vs extreme hardship
- Criminal matrix → AF / CIMT / stop-time triggers
- Positive equities for discretion

## Common pitfalls
- Using ordinary extreme-hardship template for § 240A(b) (standard is higher)
- Ignoring stop-time / presence breaks
- Seeking cancellation when reinstatement / other bars apply

## Sample language pointers
- Label LPR vs non-LPR path in the first sentence
- Hardship section must emphasize **exceptional and extremely unusual** for (b)
