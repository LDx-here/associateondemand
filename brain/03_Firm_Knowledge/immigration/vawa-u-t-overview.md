# VAWA / U / T — humanitarian overview

**Cite:** INA §§ 204(a)(1) (VAWA), 101(a)(15)(U), 101(a)(15)(T), 245(m)/(l); Forms I-360, I-918, I-914
**Practice areas:** VAWA | U visa | T visa | Humanitarian AOS

## What this element requires
- **VAWA:** battery/extreme cruelty by qualifying USC/LPR relative; good faith marriage if spouse path; residence with abuser (with exceptions); GMC; self-petition I-360 then AOS
- **U:** qualifying criminal activity; substantial physical/mental abuse; helpfulness to law enforcement; admissible or waivable (broad U waivers)
- **T):** severe form of trafficking; physically present on account of trafficking; compliance with reasonable LE requests (age/trauma exceptions); extreme hardship if removed
- These paths often bypass standard I-130 petitioner control and some § 245(c) barriers — confirm case type

## Facts that prove it
- Abuse / crime / trafficking narrative + corroboration
- Relationship / abuser status docs (VAWA)
- LE certification I-918B / I-914B where required or strategic
- Harm evidence → medical, mental health, police reports
- Admissibility issues needing I-192 / other waivers

## Common pitfalls
- Forcing AOS through abuser-controlled I-130 when VAWA is safer
- Missing derivative eligibility for children
- Under-documenting helpfulness / LE contact for U

## Sample language pointers
- Safety planning first; never serve papers in ways that endanger client
- Separate eligibility, waiver, and discretion sections
