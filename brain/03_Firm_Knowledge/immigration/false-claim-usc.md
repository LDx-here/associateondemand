# False claim to U.S. citizenship

**Cite:** INA §§ 212(a)(6)(C)(ii), 237(a)(3)(D), 237(a)(6); USCIS-PM Vol. 8
**Practice areas:** AOS | Removal | Naturalization | I-9 / benefits fraud

## What this element requires
- False representation of USC status on/after Sep 30, 1996 for any purpose/benefit under INA (incl. § 274A) or federal/state law
- Can include: border claim, I-9 “USC” box, voter registration, some licenses
- **Generally no waiver** for most applicants
- Narrow statutory exception: both parents USC (or were), permanent U.S. residence before 16, reasonable belief of citizenship
- Timely voluntary retraction may overcome in limited contexts; DOS may still discuss minor-capacity defenses — confirm current USCIS vs DOS policy

## Facts that prove it
- Exact claim → form/version, date, wording, who received it
- Knowledge / capacity → age, advice received, language barriers (policy-sensitive)
- Exception facts → parents’ citizenship; age at claim; residence history
- Voting → separate unlawful voting deportability analysis

## Common pitfalls
- Treating as waivable misrepresentation under § 212(i)
- Missing I-9 false USC claims during AOS/naturalization intake
- Relying on outdated “knowing” / under-18 USCIS policy without checking current PM

## Sample language pointers
- Flag as **strategy-critical / possibly fatal** early
- Distinguish misrepresentation (i) vs false claim (ii) in writing
