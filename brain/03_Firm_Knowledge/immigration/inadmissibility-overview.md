# Inadmissibility vs deportability — overview

**Cite:** INA §§ 212(a), 237(a); 8 USCIS-PM; AFM Ch. 40 (as incorporated)
**Practice areas:** AOS | Removal | Consular | Naturalization screening

## What this element requires
- Decide which regime applies: **inadmissibility** (§ 212) vs **deportability** (§ 237)
- § 212: seeking admission / many benefits (including AOS) — includes EWI and parolees as “seeking admission”
- § 237: inspected and **admitted** noncitizens (including many overstays)
- Same topic labels (crime, security, public charge) ≠ identical statutory tests
- Screen every matter at intake for both benefit eligibility and removal exposure

## Facts that prove it
- Entry history → inspected/admitted vs EWI vs parole
- Prior orders → removal, expedited removal, in absentia
- Criminal record → dispositions, sentences, controlled substances
- Fraud / claims → visa lies, I-9 USC claims, document misuse
- Status violations → overstay, unauthorized work, COS failures

## Common pitfalls
- Applying deportability exceptions (e.g., ≤30g marijuana) to an EWI client who is under § 212
- Treating parole as admission
- Missing form “yes/no” questions that map to § 212 grounds
- Counseling naturalization without criminal/deportability screen

## Sample language pointers
- First sentence: “Client is subject to [inadmissibility/deportability] because [entry/admission fact].”
- Map each form answer to a specific INA subsection
