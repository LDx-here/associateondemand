# Criminal grounds overview (CIMT / controlled substances)

**Cite:** INA §§ 212(a)(2), 237(a)(2); § 101(a)(48) (conviction); § 212(h); categorical approach case law
**Practice areas:** Removal | AOS | Waiver | Padilla screening

## What this element requires
- Obtain **certified dispositions** — statute of conviction, sentence, date
- Apply categorical / modified categorical approach before labeling CIMT or aggravated felony
- Distinguish **inadmissibility** vs **deportability** criminal tests (they differ)
- Controlled substances: broad § 212(a)(2)(A)(i)(II); deportability has ≤30g marijuana exception that may **not** help EWI/inadmissibility clients
- § 212(h) may waive some CIMTs / single ≤30g marijuana possession — check qualifying relatives / 15-year rehabilitation path; **not** available for many drug offenses

## Facts that prove it
- Court records → complaint, plea, judgment, sentence minutes
- Record of conviction documents for divisible statutes
- Rehabilitation → time since offense, counseling, employment
- Immigration status at offense / entry mode (drives which INA section)

## Common pitfalls
- Guessing CIMT from charge title without elements analysis
- Ignoring juvenile / vacated / expunged disposition rules (jurisdiction-specific)
- Advising plea without Padilla-quality immigration screen
- Assuming § 212(h) covers drug trafficking
