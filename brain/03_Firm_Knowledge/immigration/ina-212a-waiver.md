# INA § 212(a) waivers — common paths

**Cite:** INA §§ 212(h), 212(i), 212(a)(9)(B)(v), 212(d)(11), 212(a)(9)(A)(iii); Form I-601 / I-601A / I-212; 9 USCIS-PM
**Practice areas:** Waiver | AOS | Consular

## What this element requires
- Identify exact § 212(a) ground(s) before picking a waiver
- Meet statutory qualifying-relative / rehabilitation / equities test for that waiver
- Separate application (I-601, I-601A provisional UL presence, or I-212 consent to reapply)
- All waivers are **discretionary** even if extreme hardship is shown

## Facts that prove it
- Ground triggered → convictions, misrepresentation facts, UL presence timeline, prior removal
- Qualifying relative → status proof + relationship docs
- Extreme hardship packet → see `extreme-hardship-factors.md`
- Rehabilitation / equities → employment, taxes, community, remorse, counseling
- I-601A only → UL presence bar anticipated; **no other** ground that consulate will find

## Common pitfalls
- Seeking waiver for **non-waivable** grounds (e.g., most false USC claims; drug trafficking; many § 212(a)(9)(C) cases until 10 years abroad + I-212)
- Wrong form (I-601 vs I-601A vs I-212)
- Filing I-601A when other inadmissibility grounds exist
- Skipping discretionary equities after proving hardship

## Sample language pointers
- Open with ground + waiver authority cite
- Hardship section + discrete discretion section
- Flag non-waivable issues for attorney strategy call
