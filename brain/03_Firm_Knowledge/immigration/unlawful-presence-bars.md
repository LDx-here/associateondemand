# Unlawful presence bars — § 212(a)(9)(B)/(C)

**Cite:** INA §§ 212(a)(9)(B), 212(a)(9)(C); 8 USCIS-PM; AFM 40.9 (as incorporated)
**Practice areas:** AOS | Consular | Waiver | Provisional I-601A

## What this element requires
- **§ 212(a)(9)(B):** UL presence >180 days & <1 year → **3-year** bar after departure; ≥1 year → **10-year** bar after departure
- Bar triggers on **departure** (and seeking admission) — not while remaining in U.S.
- UL presence: overstay; or EWI; or status violation **after** official determination
- Key **exceptions** (B only): under 18; pending asylum (no unauthorized work); TPS/DED/DACA/deferred action; properly filed AOS; timely nonfrivolous EOS/COS; others per PM
- **§ 212(a)(9)(C) “permanent bar”:** aggregate >1 year UL presence **or** prior removal, then enter/attempt EWI — generally needs **10 years abroad + I-212**; B exceptions do **not** apply (minors accrue for C)

## Facts that prove it
- Timeline → each entry/exit, authorized stay end date, EWI dates
- Status determinations → NTA, USCIS denial finding status violation
- Exception evidence → age, TPS, pending I-485/I-589, DACA
- Prior orders → expedited removal, IJ order, reinstatement risk (§ 241(a)(5))

## Common pitfalls
- Advising consular processing without modeling 3/10-year trigger
- Assuming minor exception applies to § 212(a)(9)(C)
- Confusing unauthorized work start date with UL presence start (often overstay date unless official determination earlier)
- Ignoring Arrabally/Yerrabelly / advance parole travel issues — verify current USCIS policy before travel advice

## Sample language pointers
- Build a dated UL presence chart
- State whether client can remain and adjust vs must depart with I-601A
