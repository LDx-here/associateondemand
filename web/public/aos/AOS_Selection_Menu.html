I. Legal Standard

This section is verbatim in every AOS brief. It states the governing legal framework: INA §245(a), Matter of Patel (burden), Matter of Marin (balancing test), Matter of Arai (baseline rule), and the USCIS Policy Manual. No editing required — it will be inserted exactly as written below.

Verbatim — No edits required
Section 245 of the Immigration and Nationality Act provides that the Attorney General may, in his discretion, adjust the status of an alien to that of a lawful permanent resident if: (1) the alien makes an application for such adjustment; (2) the alien is eligible to receive an immigrant visa and is admissible for permanent residence; and (3) an immigrant visa is immediately available at the time the application is filed.

The grant of an application for adjustment of status under section 245 is "a matter of administrative grace." An applicant bears "the burden of showing that discretion should be exercised in his favor." USCIS exercises this discretion by balancing the totality of the circumstances, weighing favorable and adverse factors to determine whether a grant of adjustment "appears in the best interests of this country."

The Board has identified favorable factors including: family ties within the United States; residence of long duration; evidence of hardship to the respondent and family if adjustment is denied; a history of employment; evidence of value and service to the community; and other evidence attesting to good character. Adverse factors include the nature and circumstances of the inadmissibility ground, additional immigration violations, and any criminal record.

"[I]n the absence of adverse factors, adjustment will ordinarily be granted, still as a matter of discretion." Where adverse factors are not serious, the applicant need not demonstrate "unusual or outstanding equities" — that heightened standard applies only where negative factors are significant.
Citations: INA §245(a) | Matter of Patel, 17 I&N Dec. 597 (BIA 1980) | Matter of Marin, 16 I&N Dec. 581 (BIA 1978) | Matter of Arai, 13 I&N Dec. 494 (BIA 1970) | 1 USCIS-PM E.8(A)
